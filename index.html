About / motivation

From finding information to using it well

Search, recommendation, and Web agents work with information spread across documents, interaction histories, knowledge repositories, and tools. A useful agent must identify relevant evidence, preserve what matters, revise outdated beliefs, and make decisions with the context it has.

These capabilities are interdependent: reasoning can trigger retrieval; new evidence can reveal a stale memory; and memory organization affects what can be found later. This workshop brings together researchers and practitioners to study those connections and how to evaluate them over sustained interactions.

The research lens

Three capabilities, one evolving context

01 / ACQUIRE

Retrieval

Find relevant evidence and prior experience across heterogeneous, dynamic sources.

02 / MAINTAIN

Memory

Decide what persists, what changes, and how knowledge remains useful across tasks.

03 / ACT

Reasoning

Use available context to select actions, identify information needs, and verify outcomes.

Topics

What we hope to discuss

Contributions may address a single capability or the interfaces among retrieval, memory, and reasoning.

Dynamic evidence & knowledge

Agentic search, RAG, structured memory, temporal updates, and provenance.

Long-horizon agents

Planning, tool use, personalization, continual learning, and multi-agent collaboration.

Context in many forms

Multimodal inputs, context selection and compression, external memory, and knowledge graphs.

Evaluation & deployment

Reliability, privacy, resource efficiency, benchmarks, and deployed search and recommendation agents.
